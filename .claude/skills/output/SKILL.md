# Output

The user reads the result, not the process.

## Gate

Every substantive reply is a visual; the only exception is a direct one-line answer to a direct one-line question. The visual is the answer, not an illustration of a prose answer: if the reply still makes sense with the visual removed, it is prose and fails. A caption inside the visual is part of it; prose after it is a repeat.

## One idea

- The visual carries the outcome or the decision. It stands alone; no sentence repeats it.
- One visual, one idea. A second idea belongs in another output.
- Words outside the visual carry only what it cannot show: a link, an unverified caveat, or the one decision the user must make.

## Make it visible

Start from the relationship, not a chart name. Ask what changes between the marks: position on a shared scale, distance, area, time, direction, containment or connection. Choose the encoding that makes that difference visible; derive the marks from the data. Do not pick from a catalog of chart types or copy a stock template.

If removing color and labels leaves the same information as a list of sentences, it is not a visualization. If the reader has to read prose to find the point, rebuild it. Treat prose as a comment: when you want to explain the visual, put the fact on the mark instead.

- Color encodes a category, state or value with a legend; one accent for the selected path; no decorative color.
- Interaction reveals detail without hiding the overview: hover or focus highlights connected marks and shows exact values; click pins. No tabs, hidden panes or separate detail cards.
- One visual, one screen at the thread's width. If it needs a second visual, it is two outputs.

## Budget

- One screen at the thread's width; a plan card may take two.
- Cut anything that repeats the visual, describes the process or already lives in the PR.

## Design

- Use the app's tokens, font, radius and neutrals; never introduce a second palette.
- Fill the thread's width; only a list keeps a narrow column.
- Real content and one worked example. No gradients, shadows, glow or animation.

## Check

1. Render at 728px; the page fills the frame.
2. Hover and focus every mark; the tooltip and highlight work.
3. Every hue has a meaning in the legend.
4. Cover the text: the visual alone answers what changed, what to do and the one thing. If not, fix the visual; do not add a line.
5. If the visual disappears when color is removed, or the reply is still prose with a picture, rebuild.

If the page cannot be rendered, use a Mermaid diagram or a compact GFM flow, never prose.
