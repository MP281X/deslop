# Deploy intent

- One page: the machines are the bar's section control, each its icon in its machine colour and its name (dev spring blue, desktop autumn yellow); an unreachable machine shows a hollow dot beside its name, not online or offline text or a warning paragraph, and a reachable one shows no dot. The control is hidden when only one machine is known. The bar keeps the account and carries the range as stock tabs on every width; a phone shows the brand icon, the machines, the short range in its controls and the account. No page title, panel headers, search field or command palette.
- Below the bar a wide screen has two panes: the machine's apps in a ~340px list, resizable, beside the chosen app's processes under the machine's charts. A phone shows the charts above the list, one row edge to edge, and pushes an app's processes full-screen with Back in the bar. The URL holds the machine, the chosen app, the phone's pane and the range. No machine filter or Monitor page.
- Every column's content sits on the bar's 16px gutter, so row marks, chart headings and process marks share one left edge.
- Rows are stock 32px Items, 44px on a phone, with a 16px mark, the name, the trailing fact, then the one end slot. The list opens with an `ItemListHeading` "Processes", a row-high 32px band (44px on a phone) carrying a 16px stack icon, above the running apps; its text shares the charts' 32px title baseline across the split. A failing row carries a dot and a muted one-line reason after its name; a healthy row has neither. Apps show each port: exposed `outline` opening its endpoint, exposable `ghost` opening its Expose dialog, not exposable plain muted text; the three stay distinct on the selected row's muted fill, and ports are never cut. Scheduled jobs sit under one quiet row-high `ItemListHeading` with a 16px clock icon, immediately after the last app row's divider, with no gap above. A job shows when a timer next starts it, or a failure dot and how long ago, and its end slot is a play button that runs it now; a running job's end slot is only a spinner, with no running text or live dot. Failures first, then apps by memory heaviest first, then the scheduled jobs, which their clock time and play button tell apart. The whole row opens its content, its selected background is the stock muted one, and its context menu removes an endpoint this app exposed.
- The detail shows Memory, CPU and Disk as three equal charts side by side on a wide screen, their names and current figures in one 32px title band with a bottom divider, aligned with the list's Processes heading; the chart bodies start below it. A phone stacks them as 64px charts, each with the same 32px title band and its figure right-aligned, and one time axis under the last. Memory and CPU stack the machine's bands on 0–100%; Disk is the machine's used space over time as one band. No Memory|CPU tabs and no metric in the URL. Time-axis labels are HTML under each chart, never SVG text stretched by a non-uniform scale. Hovering a chart shows a stock tooltip with the band's name, time and value and a vertical cursor; the hovered band lights its row and process, and hovering a row or process lights its band, on every chart. A selected row's band stays lit. No per-core charts, network or disk I/O charts, or per-app % or MB numbers.
- The Expose dialog's title is the app's mark, its name and the port in a chip; it asks only who reaches it, as full-width Tailnet and Public tabs over the fixed tailnet URL or a typed public hostname; Cancel and Expose sit in the footer.
- A sleeping worker must not delay local monitoring or the page shell. Show cached history immediately, then refresh workers with bounded deadlines and explicit offline states.
- Every app the range recorded keeps its band, stopped ones too, named by its unit when no row is left. Scheduled jobs have no band. Apps' memory and CPU come from their cgroups, so processes that came and went between samples still count; what a user service starts in its `app-<name>.slice`, like T3 Code's agents, counts as that service. Apps' memory shrinks to fit inside the machine's used memory. Bands are each Deslop app in its icon hue, every other app a zinc step, and the small apps and the rest of the machine two darker greys. One range control (1m, 10m, 1h, 24h, 7d) for every machine — stock tabs in the bar, and the phone's shorter 1h/24h/7d line in the same controls with any opened range kept in view — with clock-aligned axes; unrecorded time, and a sample missing its reading, stays blank.
- Sample every second and save every 30 seconds and on shutdown. Retain second samples for one minute, 10-second samples for ten minutes, minute samples for one hour, quarter-hour samples for one day and hourly samples for one week; a merged sample, and a chart point, averages its bucket. Each sample records each core, the memory and CPU of every service and container holding at least 1 MiB, and the machine's used disk space, summed over its mounts. Disk history starts with this release; there is no migration or backfill. A sleeping worker never slows another's refresh. Browser, worker and MCP responses return at most 60 points regardless of range, with mounts and load once.
- An app's processes always show fully expanded, nested by indentation alone, with no dividers between rows: heaviest first, each with its 16px mark, no chevrons or accordion. A parent row with visible children stays pinned under its own ancestors while the pane scrolls, on the pane's background. A parent that only wraps one parent reads `parent › child`, and processes under 2% of the app's memory and half a percent of CPU read muted; processes never fold into a group. Each row is 32px, 44px on a phone, and its memory figure sits right-aligned in one fixed column, the unit (MiB) on hover and in the accessible name, not repeated in every row. The name comes before the arguments preview: the preview takes the remaining width and truncates first, and the name truncates only when it alone overflows. Each process shows a muted, truncated preview of its arguments to tell same-named processes apart; secret-looking flags, variables, long opaque strings and URL credentials are masked on the worker. PID and PPID remain internal; never read environment. Omit nameless listeners and ordinary system-service noise. Marks are transparent: canonical Deslop glyphs and software marks installed through SVGL's shadcn registry.
- Icons are 16px everywhere: row marks, process marks, headings and states. Consistent row heights, control heights and chart baselines on phone and desktop. Meaningful information is visible without expanding records.
- Center icon and dot boxes on the same row axis; error detail uses the same destructive color as its dot and failed actions.
- A failed service says why it stopped and how long ago, not its description.
- Stale data shows once per page: one `Problem` in the bar's controls before the range, aged from the older of the inventory and history readers, with one retry that refreshes both. Inventory and history load and recover independently; neither reader replaces usable content from the other. An unreachable machine is only a status dot on its machine tab, with no problem popover in a pane. Retain the last authorized snapshot and history during ordinary outages. An authorized empty history is empty, not loading. An authorization refusal invalidates retained data and replaces all panes with the content-area error or sign-in state. `/monitor` redirects to the page.
