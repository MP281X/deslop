# Deploy intent

- One page, grouped by machine: each machine's header (icon, name, cores, memory, disk), its memory and CPU by app over time, a line per core, then its apps one line each. Machine colours follow DESIGN.md; an unavailable machine shows a hollow dot beside its name, not online/offline text or a warning paragraph. No machine filter, Monitor page or tabs.
- A port nobody reaches yet is a hairline badge on its app's row; clicking one that can be exposed opens its Expose dialog. Exposed endpoints fill with the machine's hue, with no icon; a tailnet one reads as its port, a public one as its hostname. No Expose button or command. App rows are one line: mark, name, square endpoint badges, the app's swatch from the charts, status dot, process chevron, the whole line opening the processes; failing apps and jobs sort first, then apps by memory, heaviest first, then scheduled jobs, muted with their next run; a job's play button, where other rows keep their chevron, runs it now, for a timer-started service only. Every app keeps its own row; none fold into a group. Removing an endpoint this app exposed sits in the row's context menu. No raw peer list, protocol labels, image tags, per-app % or MB numbers, or detached details panel.
- The Expose dialog names the port in its title and asks only who reaches it, as full-width Tailnet and Public tabs over the fixed tailnet URL or a typed public hostname; Cancel and Expose sit in the footer.
- A sleeping worker must not delay local monitoring or the page shell. Show cached history immediately, then refresh workers with bounded deadlines and explicit offline states.
- Charts span the whole machine, 0–100%, so they never rescale and small loads stay small; a dashed line marks half and no scale labels repeat it. Memory and CPU stack by app, heaviest first; apps never above 5% of the machine share one grey band, and the rest of the machine's use another. Every app the range recorded keeps its band, stopped ones too, named by its unit when no row is left. Hovering a band or a row lights both. Apps' memory and CPU come from their cgroups, so processes that came and went between samples still count; what a user service starts in its `app-<name>.slice`, like T3 Code's agents, counts as that service. Apps' memory shrinks to fit inside the machine's used memory. A third chart shows disk space used. The time axis sits under the time charts; cores follow it as one small unlabeled line chart each across the machine's width. One range toggle (1m, 10m, 1h, 24h, 7d) for every machine, with clock-aligned axes; unrecorded time, and a sample missing its reading, stays blank. No network or disk I/O charts.
- Sample every second and save every 30 seconds and on shutdown. Retain second samples for one minute, 10-second samples for ten minutes, minute samples for one hour, quarter-hour samples for one day and hourly samples for one week; a merged sample, and a chart point, averages its bucket. A sleeping worker never slows another's refresh. Each sample records each core and the memory and CPU of every service and container holding at least 1 MiB. Browser, worker and MCP responses return at most 60 points regardless of range, with mounts and load once.
- An app's processes open as nested sections: each parent its own accordion, the first level open, heaviest first, each with its mark and a memory bar on the app's scale; a parent that only wraps one parent reads `parent › child`, and processes under 2% of the app's memory and half a percent of CPU read muted; processes never fold into a group. Each process shows a muted, truncated preview of its arguments to tell same-named processes apart; secret-looking flags, variables, long opaque strings and URL credentials are masked on the worker. PID/PPID remain internal; never read environment. Omit nameless listeners and ordinary system-service noise. Marks are transparent: canonical Deslop glyphs and software marks installed through SVGL's shadcn registry. Leave breathing room before the scrollbar.
- Consistent control heights, row columns and chart baselines on phone and desktop. Meaningful information is visible without expanding records.
- The bar's command field filters apps, ports and processes and hides the charts while it does; no count. Center icon and dot boxes on the same row axis; error detail uses the same destructive color as its dot and failed actions.
- A failed service says why it stopped and how long ago, not its description.
- After an inventory failure keep the last snapshot under an alert, with unavailable dots, so the charts stay visible; a history failure shows its own alert above the apps. Alerts show the error's message, not its trace. `/monitor` redirects to the page. Without a prior snapshot, or on an Auth failure, show the content-area error/sign-in state.
