# Deploy intent

- One page: the machines are the bar's section control, each its icon in its machine colour and its name (dev spring blue, desktop autumn yellow); an unreachable machine shows a hollow dot beside its name, not online or offline text or a warning paragraph, and a reachable one shows no dot. The bar keeps the search and the account, and carries the range as stock tabs on a wide screen; a phone shows the brand icon, the machines, the search icon and the account, with the metric tabs and the short range under it and the chart above the list. No page title and no panel headers.
- Below the bar a wide screen has two panes: the machine's apps in a ~340px list, resizable, beside the chosen app's processes under the machine's one chart. A phone shows the chart above the list, one row edge to edge, and pushes an app's processes full-screen with Back in the bar. The URL holds the machine, the chosen app, the phone's pane, the range and the metric. No machine filter or Monitor page.
- Rows are stock 48px Items: a 20px mark, the name, the trailing fact, then the one end slot. A failing row carries a dot and a muted one-line reason under its name; a healthy row has neither. Apps show each port as a stock Badge, exposed `secondary` opening its endpoint, exposable `outline` opening its Expose dialog, not exposable `ghost`; ports are never cut. A job shows when a timer next starts it, or a failure dot and how long ago, and its end slot is a play button that runs it now; a running job's end slot is only a spinner, with no running text or live dot. Failures first, then apps by memory heaviest first, then scheduled jobs, which their clock time and play button tell apart. The whole row opens its content, its selected background is the stock muted one, and its context menu removes an endpoint this app exposed.
- The detail's header is one line of stock tabs, Memory or CPU, with the disk as one figure and its used and total on the stock tooltip. One stacked area chart follows, spanning the whole machine 0–100% so it never rescales and small loads stay small; a dashed line marks half and no scale labels repeat it; the time axis sits under it. Bands are each Deslop app in its icon hue, every other app a zinc step, and the small apps and the rest of the machine two darker greys. Hovering a row lights its band and dims the rest; a selected row stays lit. No per-core charts, network or disk I/O charts, or per-app % or MB numbers.
- The Expose dialog's title is the app's mark, its name and the port in a chip; it asks only who reaches it, as full-width Tailnet and Public tabs over the fixed tailnet URL or a typed public hostname; Cancel and Expose sit in the footer.
- A sleeping worker must not delay local monitoring or the page shell. Show cached history immediately, then refresh workers with bounded deadlines and explicit offline states.
- Every app the range recorded keeps its band, stopped ones too, named by its unit when no row is left. Scheduled jobs have no band. Apps' memory and CPU come from their cgroups, so processes that came and went between samples still count; what a user service starts in its `app-<name>.slice`, like T3 Code's agents, counts as that service. Apps' memory shrinks to fit inside the machine's used memory. Disk space used is one figure, its exact total on hover, shown nowhere else. One range control (1m, 10m, 1h, 24h, 7d) for every machine — stock tabs in the bar on a wide screen, and the phone's shorter 1h/24h/7d line with any opened range kept in view — with clock-aligned axes; unrecorded time, and a sample missing its reading, stays blank.
- Sample every second and save every 30 seconds and on shutdown. Retain second samples for one minute, 10-second samples for ten minutes, minute samples for one hour, quarter-hour samples for one day and hourly samples for one week; a merged sample, and a chart point, averages its bucket. A sleeping worker never slows another's refresh. Each sample records each core and the memory and CPU of every service and container holding at least 1 MiB. Browser, worker and MCP responses return at most 60 points regardless of range, with mounts and load once.
- An app's processes open as nested sections: each parent its own accordion, the first level open, heaviest first, each with its mark; a parent that only wraps one parent reads `parent › child`, and processes under 2% of the app's memory and half a percent of CPU read muted; processes never fold into a group. Figures sit in one fixed right column with the unit (MiB) on hover and in the accessible name, not repeated in every row. Each process shows a muted, truncated preview of its arguments to tell same-named processes apart; secret-looking flags, variables, long opaque strings and URL credentials are masked on the worker. PID and PPID remain internal; never read environment. Omit nameless listeners and ordinary system-service noise. Marks are transparent: canonical Deslop glyphs and software marks installed through SVGL's shadcn registry. Leave breathing room before the scrollbar.
- Consistent row heights, control heights and chart baselines on phone and desktop. Meaningful information is visible without expanding records.
- The bar's palette lists the apps that need a look, every app, the scheduled jobs, the machine's graphs, the chosen app's processes, and the other Deslop tools; typing filters them. Its hotkeys step the rows (J, K), open the chosen app (Enter), go back (Escape), pick a machine (1–9), run the chosen job (R) and expose its first port (E). Searching is how the page moves; there is no page filter, and the leading slot and range stay put on every pane.
- Center icon and dot boxes on the same row axis; error detail uses the same destructive color as its dot and failed actions.
- A failed service says why it stopped and how long ago, not its description.
- Inventory and history load and recover independently; neither reader replaces usable content from the other. Retain the last authorized snapshot and history during ordinary outages. An authorized empty history is empty, not loading. An authorization refusal invalidates retained data and replaces all panes with the content-area error or sign-in state. `/monitor` redirects to the page.
